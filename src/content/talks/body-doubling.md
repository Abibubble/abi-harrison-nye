### What body doubling is

Body doubling, also called parallel working, is a way to help you start and finish tasks. You work alongside one or more other people, and having them there helps you focus, stay motivated, and feel accountable.

The “body double” doesn’t have to be doing the same task as you. Just having them there gives you support and a bit of outside motivation. That’s especially helpful for neurodivergent people, or anyone who finds executive function or getting started hard. You can do it in person or online, formally or informally, and it can help with distractions and putting things off.

### Why it’s catching on at work

Body doubling is becoming more popular at work, as part of a wider move towards working more collaboratively. A lot of people find it helps them focus and get more done.

In [a 2024 survey of 2,000 UK workers by PPL PRS](https://pplprs.co.uk/productivity1/uk-workers-lose-up-to-15-hours-a-week-to-distractions-totalling-74-days-every-year/), over two thirds (67%) said they felt distracted during the working day. 15% spent nearly two hours a day distracted, and 7% said they spent three hours. For a business, that adds up to a lot of lost time every year.

Body doubling comes from an idea in psychology called “social facilitation”: people tend to do better at tasks when other people are around. It’s thought to be especially helpful for neurodivergent people, particularly people with ADHD, and anyone who struggles with focus, motivation or executive function.

Body doubling can cut down distractions, and help with focus and motivation. It’s also a great way to connect with colleagues.

With more people working remotely or hybrid, people who like working around others can feel isolated. Body doubling can help with that, by giving a sense of community and connection.

### Body doubling and ADHD

ADHD brains can find it hard to direct their own attention: knowing what needs doing, but finding it really hard to start. That can make people with ADHD more likely to put things off. Having someone there, even if they’re just quietly working alongside you, gives you outside structure, accountability and momentum.

A lot of the time, people know what they need to do, but can’t get started. Having a set time to start, and seeing or hearing someone else working, can really help.

Body doubling can also help you get motivated, or stay motivated. We’re social creatures, and having someone else around can make a boring task more fun.

ADHD brains can have less motivation from the inside. But outside things, like feedback from other people, or a sense of reward, can still motivate people with ADHD.

Managing emotions can also be hard with ADHD. Sometimes emotions get so strong that they get in the way of getting things done. Complicated, difficult or triggering tasks can lead to frustration, anxiety, and suddenly wanting to give up. A body double can help you see things differently, or help you stay calm.

### It isn’t for everyone

Body doubling doesn’t work for everyone. You might end up chatting more than working.

Where you are can be distracting too. Cafés can have loud coffee machines, people talking, and music playing.

The right place won’t distract or disrupt you. Some people work best in total silence. Some need music. Some can’t cope with visual clutter. A place where you feel safe, supported and positively stimulated is best.

It’s important to learn what works for you.
