### What autism is

Before we get into the meat of everything, I want to give a little bit of information for anybody who’s coming to this completely fresh. Because there’s a lot of misinformation out there about autism, I wanted to start with a brief overview of what autism is, before we dive into the discussion about autism and gender.

In the UK, approximately 1 in 100 children and adults are estimated to have autism. That’s about 700,000 people in the UK. It’s quite a lot. Being autistic means that the brain works in a slightly different way from neurotypical people. It’s something that people are born with, and if you’re autistic, then you’re autistic for your whole life.

Autism is a spectrum, similar to a colour wheel, not a linear line like it’s sometimes depicted. The important thing is that this means that everybody with autism is different. Some autistic people need little or no support at all, whereas others may need help from a parent or a carer every day of their lives.

Autism presents in a lot of different ways. This can be linked to communicating and interacting with other people, understanding how people think or feel, and having issues with stimuli. So bright lights or loud noises may seem overwhelming or stressful, or sometimes just uncomfortable.

Currently, nobody knows what causes autism, or if it has a cause. It can affect people in the same family, so it might sometimes be passed on to a child by their parents. However, this hasn’t been proven.

### Autism and gender

So let’s move on to autism and gender. This is a particularly important topic, because autism is much more commonly diagnosed in males. Males are 3 times more likely to receive a diagnosis for autism than females are.

This is because of many things. A lot of the symptoms of autism in females mean that it can be hard to tell if you’re autistic, due to how those symptoms present. Autistic females may also have learned to hide the signs of autism to fit in with current society, and may also be quieter and hide their feelings more. This doesn’t mean that females are less likely to have autism, it just means that it presents differently. The diagnostic tests have also always been far more focused on the male presentation of autism, rather than everybody as a whole.

**Maria:** And I hit a bit of a brick wall, because at that moment, the GP said to me, “Well, you turned 18 six months ago, and therefore, you’re an adult.” It’s a bit of a postcode lottery, but in our area, there wasn’t an adult diagnosis service for women.

### The diagnostic process

**Maria:** The diagnostic process was weird. It was a very, very weird thing to go through. It was me and 2 professionals in the room. I was being filmed. There were really odd tasks to do.

The idea behind that is that children and adults with autism can’t possibly have imagination.
