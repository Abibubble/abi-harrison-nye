### Slide 4

The heading is “What it actually is…”, with two points:

- “OCD is a mental health condition characterized by unwanted, intrusive thoughts (obsessions)”
- “The need to complete repetitive, time-consuming behaviors or rituals (compulsions), which are
  performed to temporarily relieve the obsession”

Below the list: “This cycle can cause the affected person anxiety and extreme discomfort and severely
disrupt daily life”.

### Slide 5

The heading is “The OCD Cycle”. Under it is a diagram from Choosing Therapy, with its own title “The
OCD Cycle” and the Choosing Therapy logo in the bottom left corner. The diagram is a circle split into
four coloured quarters, numbered 1 to 4. Curved arrows around the edge go clockwise from one quarter
to the next, and from the last quarter back to the first, so the cycle starts again.

1. “Obsession” (red, top left): “Unwanted, recurring intrusive thoughts that cause distress. The
   content of the obsession varies depending on the type of OCD.”
2. “Anxiety” (orange, top right): “Obsessions cause fear that results in anxiety. You then feel the
   need to do something to lessen the anxiety, leading to compulsions.”
3. “Compulsion” (blue, bottom right): “Compulsions may be observable behaviors or mental rituals,
   such as checking locks, excessive hand-washing, or reassurance-seeking.”
4. “Temporary relief” (green, bottom left): “Because there is relief after a compulsion, these
   behaviors become reinforced. Relief is short-lived until the obsession occurs again.”

### Slide 15

The heading is “Support and management”. SSRIs are selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors, a type
of antidepressant. The slide reads: “OCD cannot be cured, but can be managed for the duration of a
person’s life, the most common management techniques:”

- “Cognitive Behavioural Therapy (CBT)”
  - “Low-intensity therapy designed to help target obsessions / triggers and teach individuals the
    techniques to manage these on a daily basis”
- “Exposure and Response Prevention (ERP) therapy”
  - “A more intense therapy that aims to treat obsessions by exposing the individual to them, and
    building comfort / relief over time”
- “Medication”
  - “Often a last resort, SSRIs (also used for depression) are the most common medication provided to
    help manage OCD, usually as a short-term measure”
